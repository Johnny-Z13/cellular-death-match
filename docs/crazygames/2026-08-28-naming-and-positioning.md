# CrazyGames Naming And Release Positioning

Date: 2026-08-28

Status: working release decision; preliminary collision screen complete; formal trademark clearance still required.

## Decision

Use **Cellular Death Match** as the CrazyGames release title.

Keep **Death Match** as two words. The three-word construction preserves the double reading of *cellular death* and *death match*. Changing it to **Cellular Deathmatch** would make the title feel more like a conventional PvP mode without improving its fit with the ecosystem-cultivation loop.

The title is strong because it is:

- Memorable and easy to arrange as a three-line logo.
- Immediately associated with microscopic conflict.
- Distinct from nearby cell-game names currently visible on CrazyGames.
- Preliminarily unique in exact-phrase web and game-store searches.

The main risk is promise alignment. **Death Match** suggests immediate arena combat or PvP, while the game's distinctive player fantasy is cultivating, breeding, destabilising, and balancing a living ecosystem. CrazyGames' quality guidance says a game's name and imagery should accurately reflect the experience and should not be easily confused with another game. Release metadata and cover art therefore need to communicate the cultivation layer immediately.

## Positioning Statement

> Cultivate a living ecosystem in a volatile petri dish. Breed strains, combine reagents, and hold homeostasis before the culture collapses.

Short marketing line for title screens, trailers, social posts, or other placements that permit supporting copy:

> Cultivate. Mutate. Survive.

Do not place the marketing line on CrazyGames cover images. CrazyGames permits the game title on covers but prohibits additional calls to action or other text.

## CrazyGames Metadata Direction

### Short description

Cultivate a living ecosystem in a volatile petri dish. Seed competing lifeforms, combine reagents, discover new strains, and keep the culture alive as pressure escalates.

### Longer description

Cellular Death Match is an ecosystem-cultivation roguelike played inside a living petri dish. Seed lifeforms, feed or repel colonies with lab tools, combine reagents to trigger catalytic reactions, and cross-breed discovered strains into new hybrids. Each trial grows more unstable until the ecosystem collapses or reaches visible homeostasis.

You do not directly command every organism. Instead, you shape conditions and watch competition, blooms, mutation, and collapse emerge from the simulation. Discoveries persist in the strain library and expand the loadouts available in future runs.

### Genre and feature language

Lead with:

- Ecosystem roguelike
- Petri-dish simulation
- Breed and discover strains
- Combine reagents
- Cultivate toward homeostasis
- Escalating run pressure

Avoid leading with:

- Deathmatch
- PvP
- Arena battler
- Direct-control combat
- Idle or clicker

These terms would attract players expecting a substantially different core loop.

## Cover Direction

The cover needs to reconcile the title's combat energy with the game's real interaction model.

- Show several visibly different cultures colliding or competing inside one dish.
- Make reagent or pipette intervention visible so the player understands that this is a lab experiment.
- Use distinct colony colours and silhouettes rather than a single anonymous cell.
- Preserve the dark bioluminescent laboratory identity.
- Keep the title large enough to read on a small mobile card.
- Use only the game title as cover text.
- Avoid imagery that resembles a conventional shooter, character brawler, or two-player versus screen.

The existing `public/art/title-keyart-1024.png` establishes the dark dish atmosphere, but release covers should make competing cultures and player intervention more explicit.

## Preliminary Collision Screen

This is a product-naming screen, not legal clearance.

### Current title

- Exact searches for **Cellular Death Match** surfaced the project's own Z13 presence and no competing game title.
- Exact searches for **Cellular Deathmatch** did not surface an existing game title.
- Nearby CrazyGames titles include **Cell Survivor**, **Boom Cell**, and **Bacteria**. The current title remains recognisably different from all three.

### Names to avoid

- **Petri Panic**: already used by a closely adjacent petri-dish game released on Steam in August 2026, as well as an older WarioWare microgame.
- **Cell Lab**: established organism-design simulation.
- **CellCraft**: established browser biology game.
- **Homeostasis**: existing Steam game title.
- **Culture Shock**: heavily occupied across games and entertainment, including a Sam & Max episode.
- **Cell Survivor**: current CrazyGames title in the same broad microscopic roguelite space.

## Alternative Shortlist

| Rank | Name | Strength | Main weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cellular Death Match | Most memorable, clickable, and preliminarily distinctive | Slightly overpromises direct combat |
| 2 | Unstable Cultures | Accurate biological pun that expresses the run structure | Less immediately recognisable as a cell game |
| 3 | Petri Protocol | Short, premium, and brandable | Sounds more like a puzzle or narrative mission |
| 4 | Cellular Alchemy | Strong fit for reagents, mutation, and cross-breeding | Phrase already has substantial scientific usage |
| 5 | Culture Collapse | Communicates tension and the fail state | Search meaning leans toward society and music |
| 6 | Petri Eden | Communicates cultivation and living ecosystems | Calmer than the escalating-pressure experience |
| 7 | Strainbound | Distinctive and extensible | Meaning is opaque without artwork or description |

**Unstable Cultures** is the strongest fallback if later testing shows that the current name attracts the wrong audience. It is a better description of the game, but likely weaker at earning an initial CrazyGames click.

## Pre-Release Clearance Checklist

Before submission:

1. Search **Cellular Death Match**, **Cellular Deathmatch**, **Cellular Death**, and close phonetic variants in the [UKIPO trade mark search](https://www.gov.uk/search-for-trademark).
2. Repeat the search in the [WIPO Global Brand Database](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database) and relevant regional registers for intended release territories.
3. Review software and entertainment goods/services, especially Nice Classes 9 and 41, with qualified counsel if a close mark appears.
4. Confirm the desired domain and social handles before commissioning final locked title assets.
5. Recheck major game stores immediately before submission because new titles can appear after this research date.

WIPO notes that its global database does not replace searches of relevant national or regional registers. This document should not be treated as legal advice or as a completed trademark opinion.
